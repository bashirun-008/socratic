Circuits cheat sheet
Ohm's law

V = I R. Voltage across a resistor equals current times resistance.

Series and parallel

Series: same current through every resistor, voltages add, R_eq = R1 + R2 + ...

Parallel: same voltage across every branch, currents add, 1/R_eq = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + ... For three resistors of 10, 12 and 15 ohm in parallel, 1/R_eq = 1/10 + 1/12 + 1/15 = 1/4, so R_eq is 4 ohm.

Ideal meters

Ideal voltmeter: infinite resistance, draws no current, acts as an open circuit. It reads the potential difference between the two points it touches. Ideal ammeter: zero resistance, acts as a wire, connected in series.

Kirchhoff's laws

KCL: the sum of currents entering a junction equals the sum leaving it. KVL: the sum of voltage changes around any closed loop is zero.

Current division

For parallel branches the current in branch k is I_total times (1/R_k) divided by the sum of all the 1/R. The smallest resistance carries the largest current.

Common mistakes

Treating an ideal voltmeter like a wire. Forgetting that a resistor in series with an ideal voltmeter carries no current. Adding parallel resistances directly instead of adding their reciprocals.
